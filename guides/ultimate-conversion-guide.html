The Ultimate Oven to Air Fryer Conversion Guide

9 min read • Updated August 2026

Air fryers have become one of the most popular kitchen appliances because they cook food faster and with less oil than traditional ovens. However, most recipes are still written for conventional ovens. This guide explains exactly how to convert oven recipes so they work well in an air fryer.

Why You Need to Convert Recipes

Air fryers use rapid hot air circulation. This is more efficient than a regular oven, which means food cooks faster and usually at a slightly lower temperature. If you use the exact same temperature and time as an oven recipe, the food often turns out dry, overcooked, or burnt on the outside.

Basic Conversion Rules

Here are the two most important rules most people follow:

  • Temperature: Reduce the oven temperature by 20–30°F (about 10–15°C).
  • Time: Reduce the cooking time by 20–25% (sometimes up to 30% for very crispy foods).

A good starting point is to lower the temperature by 25°F and reduce the time by about 25%. Then check the food a few minutes early.

Recommended Starting Adjustments

  • Oven 350°F → Air fryer ~325°F
  • Oven 375°F → Air fryer ~350°F
  • Oven 400°F → Air fryer ~375°F
  • Oven 425°F → Air fryer ~400°F

Important Tips for Best Results

  • Always preheat your air fryer if the recipe recommends it (or if your model needs it).
  • Do not overcrowd the basket. Hot air needs space to circulate.
  • Shake or flip the food halfway through cooking for even browning.
  • Check food 5–10 minutes before the calculated time is up.
  • Different air fryer models cook slightly differently — results can vary.

When to Adjust Further

Larger or thicker pieces of food (like whole chicken breasts or big roasts) may need a bit more time. Smaller or thinner items usually cook faster. Frozen foods often need less adjustment than fresh ones.
