5 Common Mistakes When Converting Oven Recipes to Air Fryer

8 min read • Updated August 2026

Converting oven recipes to the air fryer is usually straightforward, but a few common mistakes can lead to dry, soggy, or unevenly cooked food. Here are the five most frequent errors and how to avoid them.

1. Not Checking Food Early

Air fryers cook faster than ovens. If you wait until the full calculated time is up, the food is often overcooked. Always start checking 5–10 minutes before the suggested time.

2. Overcrowding the Basket

Hot air needs space to circulate. When the basket is too full, food steams instead of crisping. Cook in a single layer and work in batches if necessary.

3. Using the Exact Same Temperature

Most oven recipes need a temperature reduction of about 20–30°F (10–15°C). Using the original oven temperature often causes the outside to brown too quickly while the inside is still undercooked.

4. Forgetting to Flip or Shake

Food that sits in one position the entire time will brown unevenly. Shake the basket or flip the food halfway through cooking for better results.

5. Not Accounting for Food Size and Thickness

Thicker pieces (large chicken breasts, big roasts, dense vegetables) usually need more time than the basic conversion suggests. Thinner or smaller items cook faster. Use a meat thermometer when cooking meat.

Quick Summary

  • Lower the temperature by ~25°F
  • Reduce time by 20–25%
  • Check early
  • Don’t overcrowd
  • Flip or shake halfway

Following these simple adjustments will give you much more consistent and better-tasting results.
